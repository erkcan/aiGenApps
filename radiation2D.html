Physics of Rad2D

1. The Shintake Method

This simulation visualizes the electric field lines of a moving point charge using the algorithm proposed by Tsumoru Shintake. Unlike traditional methods that solve complex differential equations, this method treats field lines as a collection of "test points" emitted by the source.

Reference: Tsumoru Shintake, "Real-time animation of synchrotron radiation", NIMA 507 (2003) 89–92, doi:10.1016/S0168-9002(03)00844-1

Each point propagates radially at the speed of light $c$ from its emission coordinate. The angular distribution of these points is governed by Relativistic Aberration, causing the field lines to "bunch up" in the direction of motion (Searchlight effect).

2. Freeform Interaction

In Freeform mode, the particle is modeled as a physical body with Inertia. It is connected to your cursor via a virtual spring.

  • Mass & Momentum: The particle has a mass of $3.0$ units, causing it to overshoot and oscillate, which naturally generates radiation "wiggles."
  • Force Clamping: To prevent numerical instability (and field lines crossing) when clicking far away, the input force is clamped to a maximum value.
  • Velocity Smoothing: We apply a low-pass filter to the velocity used for aberration. This ensures that even under violent acceleration, the field lines swing smoothly rather than folding into unphysical shocks.
