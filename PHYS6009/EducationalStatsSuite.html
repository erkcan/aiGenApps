How this heatmap is built — pure Monte Carlo, no Beta function.

For each of the 50 values of p on the y-axis, we run 1 000 independent experiments of 14 coin flips. The fraction of experiments yielding exactly k heads fills each cell of column k — giving an empirical estimate of P(X=k | p).

CI boundary lines are found by: for each p-row, sort the simulated k outcomes and cut the lowest 2.5% and highest 2.5% tails. Then for each k, scan over p to find where k first enters / last exits that central band.

The Dutch Book Argument ▼ EXPAND

A Dutch Book is a set of bets that guarantees the bettor a loss, regardless of the outcome — a sign of incoherent credences. De Finetti proved: an agent is immune to a Dutch Book if and only if their betting odds obey the probability axioms.

The Bayesian here is coherent by construction — odds follow the posterior directly. Over many bets, they should break even.

The Sophisticated Frequentist finds the highest CL α for which her α-CI fits inside [A,B], and bets at that α. Her prices are genuinely data-informed and vary by range. But notice: in betting at α, she is implicitly treating α as a probability that θ ∈ [A,B] — precisely what Frequentism forbids. She has smuggled in a posterior through the back door.

Test Inversion never refuses. She computes the average p-value of x̄ across all θ ∈ [A,B] — every point in the range votes, weighted equally. Wide ranges are penalised because most θ values in a wide interval are far from x̄. Her prices are continuous and data-informed, but she violates monotonicity: a wider range containing a narrower one can get a lower price. This incoherence is itself a lesson.

Confidence Distribution never refuses and satisfies monotonicity. She uses H(θ) = Φ((θ−x̄)/SE) — a frequentist distributional summary — and prices any [A,B] as H(B)−H(A). Her prices are always coherent, continuous, and data-informed. But they are numerically identical to the Bayesian with a flat prior, in every single bet. This is de Finetti's theorem made concrete: a coherent Frequentist betting strategy, when it exists, is a probability measure.

Key question for seminar: Does the Frequentist's refusal to price individual intervals protect them from a Dutch Book — or expose them to one?

Caveats: The Dutch Book here is really trained against a particular agent's behaviour — Test Inversion's incoherence — yet we offer the same bets to all agents. This is a pedagogical simplification: a true Dutch Book would be tailored to the specific prices an agent announces, constructing a guaranteed loss. Here we observe tendencies, not guarantees. This simulation also slightly favors the Simple and Sophisticated Frequentists to make the game more fun and educational. Finally, the descriptions here should not be taken as solid arguments for or against a philosophical outlook — they are not even entirely sound. The goal is to help us develop our own understanding of these positions.
